# Code of Conduct

OSSGrid exists to help people take their first steps in open source. Many contributors here are making their first pull request ever. We want every one of them to leave wanting to do it again.

## Our standards

**Do:**

- Be welcoming and patient, especially with newcomers and their questions.
- Assume good intent. Ask before you judge.
- Give feedback on the work, not the person. Be specific and kind.
- Accept feedback gracefully, and say thanks when someone helps you.
- Credit other people's ideas and work.
- Respect people's time: communicate when you are blocked or need to step away.

**Don't:**

- Harass, insult or demean anyone, in public or private.
- Make jokes or comments about anyone's identity, background, ability or appearance.
- Share someone's private information without permission. This includes mentor emails that appear in source data.
- Spam issues, claim work you won't do, or pressure maintainers for reviews.
- Submit someone else's work, including copied proposals, as your own.

## Scope

This applies in the OSSGrid repository (issues, pull requests, discussions, reviews and commit messages) and anywhere you represent the project.

## Reporting

If someone's behaviour makes you uncomfortable, contact the maintainer, [@rajatrsrivastav](https://github.com/rajatrsrivastav), privately on GitHub or at [x.com/rajatrsrivastav](https://x.com/rajatrsrivastav). Reports are handled privately. You will not be penalised for reporting in good faith.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may remove comments, close issues or pull requests, unassign issues, or block people who break this code. The response depends on what happened, from a private warning to a permanent ban for serious or repeated harm.
